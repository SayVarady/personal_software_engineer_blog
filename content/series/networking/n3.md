To send and receive data through network, we need a protocol. 
That's where TCP and UDP come in. Both of these protocols are in the 4th layer of the OSI model, the Transport layer. 
However, TCP and UDP are being used in a different way depending on the how your application handle the data.
TCP provides a very reliable way to send your data over the network by ensuring your data is received correctly by the receiver.
It ensures that no data is lost or corrupted. Its trade-off is that it is much slower since it has to establish a connection between you and the receiver first. In software engineering, every network communication costs something. It is called handshakes. There are other topic that we will cover in the future about the TCP handshake. TCP attaches a sequence number to each packet it sends, so the receiver can verify that the packet is received correctly.

UDP, on the other hand, is a connectionless protocol that does not guarantee the delivery of packets. It is faster than TCP since it does not have to establish a connection first. However, it does not provide any reliability guarantees, so packets may be lost or corrupted.
